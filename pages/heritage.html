The Connection

Germany & Cameroon — A Shared History

From 1884 to 1916, present-day Cameroon was the German protectorate of Kamerun. During this era, German engineers, architects, missionaries, and administrators left a profound mark on the country — in its infrastructure, its architecture, and in the very names of its places.

The city of Limbe was originally named Victoria. It was Engineer Limburg — the German colonial engineer whose protection of the local freshwater river earned him legendary status among residents — who gave the river its name. In 1982, by presidential decree, the city of Victoria was renamed Limbe in honor of that river and its German protector.

The Limburg German Cultural Center was built on this legacy — to preserve it, share it, and use it as a foundation for genuine cultural exchange between two nations bound by history.

On-Site Discoveries

Colonial Heritage Sites

Key historical landmarks across Cameroon that tell the story of the German era.

🏛️
1884 – 1916
German Governor's Palace, Buea

The former seat of German colonial administration — an imposing hilltop building that overlooks the slopes of Mount Cameroon and remains a key historical landmark.

💡
1890s
Limbe Lighthouse

A surviving German-built lighthouse that guided ships into Victoria Harbor — one of the few intact colonial navigation structures on the West African coast.

🌿
1892
Limbe Botanic Garden

Established by the Germans as a research and agricultural station, the garden is now one of Africa's most important botanical sites — preserving thousands of plant species.

⛪
1900s
Basel Mission Houses

German missionary architecture that dots the landscape of the South West Region — built stone structures that have outlasted the colonial era and continue to serve their communities.
